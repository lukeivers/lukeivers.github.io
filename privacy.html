mail-feed privacy policy

Last updated 25 September 2026.

Who uses it

mail-feed is a personal tool run by Luke Ivers on his own computer, for his own Gmail accounts and those of members of his household who have agreed to it.

What it accesses

With an account holder's permission, given through Google's sign-in, mail-feed reads, labels, archives and drafts messages in that Gmail account, and sends messages only when the account holder approves. Where the account holder also allows it, mail-feed creates and removes Gmail filters that archive, label, mark read or star incoming mail; it never sets up forwarding.

Where data goes

Sign-in tokens are stored in the macOS Keychain on Luke's computer. mail-feed keeps a local log of the changes it makes, which records message IDs and labels but no message content. It doesn't sell, share or transfer Gmail data to anyone. Data read through mail-feed is used only to help the account holder manage their own mail, including through AI assistants they run.

Google API data

mail-feed's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

Account holders can revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/connections, and Luke can sign an account out of mail-feed, which also revokes its token.
